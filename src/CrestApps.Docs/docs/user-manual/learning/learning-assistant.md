The learning assistant brings the site's AI to your courses in three ways: a tutor for learners, an authoring assistant for course authors, and scoring help for graders. Each one only suggests or drafts. Nothing the AI writes is published, and no grade is saved until a person saves it.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Menu** | Artificial Intelligence > AI Profiles (the tutor); Learning > Authoring assistant; Learning > Grading (scoring help) |
| **Permission** | Manage AI profiles for the tutor; Use the learning authoring assistant; Use scoring help while grading |
| **Feature** | Learning Assistant; Learning Authoring Assistant for the authoring assistant and scoring help |

The site needs an AI connection and a deployment first. Without one, the pages say so and do nothing.

## Set up the tutor

1. Go to **Artificial Intelligence > AI Profiles** and select **Add Profile**.
2. Pick **Learning tutor**, give the profile a title, and create it.
3. On the profile's **Knowledge** tab, pick the AI data source that holds your lessons. **Learning tutor** is already ticked.
4. Show the profile to learners with the AI Chat widget, for example on your learning pages.
5. Open each course the tutor may answer about, tick **Let the learning tutor answer this course's learners**, and publish the course. The box is off on every course until you tick it.

What the tutor can use:

- Only the page, video and file lessons of the courses the learner is enrolled in, that are released to them, and whose tutor box is ticked. A learner who is not enrolled, whose access ended, or whose enrollment is suspended gets nothing from that course.
- Never a question, a quiz, a question bank or an answer key. This holds even when a learner asks for the answers, and on every AI profile of the site, not only the tutor.
- The learner's own progress and next lesson, and their own quiz results. A result is explained only as far as the quiz's review setting lets the learner see it: when the quiz shows the score only, the tutor gives the score and does not say which answers were right.

## AI tools for staff

Any AI profile can also use two staff tools: **Overdue learners** and **Learners whose access ends soon**. They list the learner, the course, the date and the progress, never answers or grades, and only for the learners the person may see: everyone for people with View learner records, the learners of the courses they teach, or the people they manage.

## Draft with the authoring assistant

Go to **Learning > Authoring assistant**. Each card makes drafts that you review, change and publish yourself:

- **Draft a course outline**: say what the course is about and who it is for. You get a draft course with sections and a draft page lesson for each item, and the course editor opens.
- **Draft questions from a lesson**: pick a lesson and a question bank. Draft questions with their right answers are added to the bank, unpublished. Check every answer before you publish a question.
- **Draft a rubric**: describe the work it grades. You get a draft rubric with criteria and levels.
- **Translate a lesson**: pick a lesson and a language. You get a draft copy with the translated title and text; the lesson itself does not change. Add the copy to the course outline when it is ready.

## Use scoring help while grading

1. Open a coursework submission from **Learning > Grading**.
2. Select **Suggest a score**. The AI fills in a score (or a level for each rubric criterion) and feedback, based on the rubric.
3. Read the work yourself, change anything you disagree with, and save the grade as usual.

The grade then shows **AI-assisted**, on the submission and in its history. Quiz answers, such as essays, are graded on the Surveys grading page, which has its own AI scoring help when the Surveys AI feature is on. A suggestion is never saved on its own, and a certificate or credential only ever follows the grade you save. Work handed in only as files gets no suggestion.
