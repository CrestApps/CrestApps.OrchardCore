Some answers, such as long texts and file uploads, need a person to score them.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Menu** | **Surveys** → **Grading** |
| **Permission** | Grade survey responses, or Grade responses of own surveys for your own surveys |
| **Feature** | Surveys |

<AskYourAdmin />

## Grade a response

1. Go to **Surveys** → **Grading**. The list shows the responses waiting for a grader.
2. Open a response. For each answer, read the **Guidance**, enter the **Points** and a **Comment**. When changing a given score needs more than grading (on a Learning quiz, **Override grades**) and you don't have it, you can only give points to the answers that need grading: the points of the others, scored by the site or by another grader, are read-only.
3. Enter **Overall feedback** and select **Save grades**.

With Surveys AI, **Suggest a score with AI** fills in a suggestion; you decide and save. Tick **I used AI help to grade this answer** when you used it.

Nobody grades their own work: your own responses are left out of your list, and you can't grade, change the score of or score again a response you gave. If you answered one of the versions you pick in **Regrade versions**, someone else must run it. You also can't void or delete your own response.

On an anonymous survey, responses wait in the list until the survey has enough completed responses (its minimum group size), so no one can be recognized from the first answers. They show a code instead of a number, in no particular order, and the day only. A response given through a link, or to a version, that too few others used together isn't listed and doesn't open for grading. The survey's owner gets no notice per response on an anonymous survey, since its time would show when that person handed in; check the list instead. Nobody grades their own work, but on an anonymous survey the site can't tell: an anonymous response doesn't record who gave it, so a grader who also responded can be shown their own response. Give grading of anonymous surveys to people who didn't take them.

## Change or recompute a score

- **Change the total score**: enter the **New score** and a **Reason**, then **Change score**. The reason is kept in the score history.
- **Score again**: scores the response again with the key of its version. Grader scores are kept.
- **Regrade versions** (in the survey's menu): scores the responses of older versions with the key of another version, with a **Reason**. Only questions whose type did not change take the other key. A question whose choices changed ids between the two versions (for example, a bank imported again) keeps its scores, and a message lists those questions so you can grade them by hand.
