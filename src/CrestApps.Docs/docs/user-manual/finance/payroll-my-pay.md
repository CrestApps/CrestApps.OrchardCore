**My pay** is your own pay: the statements of every employer that pays you, their stubs, your tax elections and where your net pay goes. It is the **Pay** section of your own staff profile. You see only your own pay, never anyone else's.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Menu** | Staff > My Profile > **My pay** (opens the **Pay** section of your profile) |
| **Permission** | View own pay; Manage own pay details to change your elections and where your pay goes |
| **Feature** | My Pay |

<AskYourAdmin />

## Confirm your password

Pay is private: before showing it, the **Pay** section asks for your password. Choose **Confirm your password**, type it and choose **Confirm**. For the next 10 minutes your pay pages open without asking again; after that, or on another browser, you are asked again. If you sign in only with an outside account, set a password on your profile first.

## See your pay statements

Open **My pay**. **Pay statements** lists your statements, the newest first, with the pay date, the employer and your net pay. **Open** shows a statement: each line with its hours and amount, gross and net pay, and how your net pay was paid (by deposit to which account, or by which check). **Stub** or **Download the stub** gives the statement as a PDF, with the year's totals; **Print** prints the statement page.

A statement shows once your pay is processed. A statement marked **Voided** was cancelled by a reversing statement, which is listed too; your payroll contact pays what is owed in a new statement.

If the page says your user is not linked to a staff member, ask your payroll contact to link it.

Once your employer files the year's wage statements, **Tax forms** lists yours with a PDF: Form W-2 or 1099-NEC in the United States, the T4 slip in Canada, and the RL-1 slip for work in Quebec.

## Review your coming pay

Where your employer asks for it, you're told when your pay is calculated, before it is approved. **My pay** then shows **Your coming pay**: click **Review** to see the pay statement as it stands, with its lines, your net pay and your time-off balances. Click **This looks right**, or write what looks wrong (such as missing hours) and click **Ask about it**: payroll sees your question on the pay run before it is submitted. If your pay changes after you looked, you're told again. Once the run is submitted, the pay can no longer be reviewed.

## Ask an assistant about your pay

If your site has an AI assistant with the payroll tools, ask it to explain your pay statement, such as "Why is my pay lower this time?". It reads only your own statements, compares the latest with the one before, and never sees your tax id or bank account numbers.

## Change your tax election or where your pay goes

Under **Your pay details**, choose **Tax elections and where your pay goes** for the employer:

1. To add a tax election, choose the jurisdiction, the day it counts from (today by default) and fill in its values. It applies to pay that is not approved yet.
2. Under **Net pay**, choose how your pay is paid, in order: a deposit to one of your bank accounts or a check, for an amount, a percent or what is left. Pay is deposited only to a verified account of yours: add or change your accounts under **My bank accounts**.
3. Choose **Save**.

Every change to your elections or to where your pay goes sends you a notice, even one your payroll contact made. If you did not ask for it, tell your payroll contact at once.
