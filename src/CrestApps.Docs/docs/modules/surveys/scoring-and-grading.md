Any survey can be scored; a quiz is a survey of kind `Quiz` with scoring on.

## Scoring settings

`SurveyDefinition.Scoring`: `Enabled`, `PassMarkPercent`, `DefaultPoints`, `NegativeMarking`, `ShowScoreToRespondent`, `FeedbackMode` (`Deferred` or `Immediate`), `RoundingDecimals` and `ScoreFormula` (the sum of the points when empty).

A score formula reads the response being scored: `[score:category]` for a category's total, `SCORE('question name')` for a question's points, and `[score]` and `[percent]` for the plain sum of the points and its percent of the maximum, before the formula and before rounding (`ISurveyScoreFormulaEvaluator`). For example, `[score:reading] * 2 + [score:writing]` counts reading twice.

`RoundingDecimals` rounds the score and the percent that are stored and shown. The pass mark and the score band are decided on the unrounded percent, so 69.5% does not pass a 70% pass mark at 0 decimals.

Points per choice (`ChoicePoints`) may be negative. Like a penalty, a negative choice takes points away only with negative marking on; otherwise the question scores at least 0, as multiple choice does. A Ranking question with **Items to rank** set is scored on that many places: its key is the first that many items of the right order (or of the designed order when it has no key), so ranking them all right gets full credit.

A question's key (`SurveyQuestion.Scoring`) holds `Points` (empty uses the default; 0 leaves it unscored), `PartialCredit`, `PenaltyPerWrong`, `CategoryId`, `ChoicePoints`, `CorrectChoiceIds`, `AcceptedAnswers`, `CaseSensitive`, `NumberAnswer` and `Tolerance`, matching `Pairs`, feedback texts per answer and per choice, `GraderGuidance`, and `Key` for custom types. The key never reaches respondents.

## How a response is scored

`ISurveyScoringEngine` scores every question the respondent was shown with its type's scorer when the response is completed:

- an unanswered question scores 0; a question that logic hid, or that sits on a page a branch skipped, does not count toward the maximum, unless its answer is locked (see below);
- a scorer that returns no points (a long text, a file upload) leaves the response **Pending** a grader;
- category totals, the score (or the score formula), the percent, the pass mark and the score band are set;
- a grader's score always wins over the automatic one.

The completion scores the response as completed, so it counts the pages on the respondent's page path (`SurveyResponse.PagePath`), the same questions a later regrade and the review count; this holds for a response submitted at its deadline too. Scoring runs before the ending is chosen, so an ending condition on `score` or `score:{categoryId}` reads this response's score; a branch that goes to an ending names it directly. Reopening a response (edit after submit) clears its score, percent, pass and band, so rules never read an earlier completion's score.

A response that still waits for a grader never passes, and its score, right answers and feedback stay hidden from the respondent until it is graded.

## Review policy

`SurveyDefinition.Review` sets what respondents see (score, their answers, right answers, answer feedback, general feedback) at each moment: while answering (immediate feedback), right after finishing, later while the survey is open, and after it closes. `ResultsVisibility` decides when respondents see the totals of all responses (polls). `ISurveyReviewPolicy` applies these on the review page, the ending and immediate feedback.

A scored survey that lets respondents change a finished response (`Navigation.AllowEditAfterSubmit`) can't show the score (when `ShowScoreToRespondent` is on), the right answers or answer feedback right after finishing or later while the survey is open: reopens are unlimited, so the respondent could read the key, or change one answer at a time and watch the score, and resubmit. The validator refuses that combination (`edit-after-review`). The ending gives the score away the same way, so with changes after finishing on, the validator also refuses an ending that shows the score (`{score}`, `{percent}`, `{score:category}`, or Liquid `Score`, `Response.Percent` or `Response.Passed`, in a message, a translated message or a redirect URL), and a conditional ending chosen by the score (a rule on `score` or `score:{categoryId}`, or a formula reading `[score]`, `[percent]`, `[score:category]`, `SCORE(...)` or a calculated variable built on them) (`edit-after-score-ending`). Whatever the settings, an ending shows the score, the percent, the category scores and the pass only when the review at that moment shows the respondent the score (`ISurveyReviewPolicy`, which also follows `ShowScoreToRespondent` and keeps the score hidden while a response waits for a grader); otherwise those references render empty. Show them after the survey closes, when nothing can be reopened, or turn off changes after finishing. The closing date counts as a close: from then on nothing is reopened and responses in progress are submitted as they were saved ([When a survey closes](./collecting.md#when-a-survey-closes)). The review after close, and the totals of all responses shown after close, start once the closing date and its grace (`GraceSeconds`) are over (`SurveyAvailability.IsClosedForReview`), since a last page posted at the close is still taken until then; a close by staff starts them at once. A learner whose accommodation closes later can still answer after the survey's close, while classmates may already see the review after close, as in Moodle: give such a quiz a review after close only when that is acceptable, or set the survey's closing date after the last accommodation's.

With **One at a time, then locked** (`FeedbackMode.Immediate`), the respondent checks an answer (`/s/r/{responseId}/check/{questionId}`); it is scored and locked, and the feedback the review allows during the response is shown.

A locked answer (`SurveyAnswer.CheckedUtc` is set; `SurveyAnswer.IsLocked`) is never removed or changed. The respondent also can't take it off their path or hide it: a save, a page post (Next, Back or Submit), a check or an API page post whose answers would do that is refused with `SurveyErrorCode.InvalidAnswer`, nothing is saved, and the error ("You can't change this answer now...") goes on each posted answer that would do it. To decide, the runtime walks the response from its first page with its answers before and after the write, choosing each next page as a page post does (page visibility, branches, branch endings, the answers a branch skips dropped, calculated variables recomputed), and compares which locked answers end up on a page of that path and not hidden. Live evaluation (`/s/r/{responseId}/evaluate`) returns the same errors in `errors`, so the page says so as the respondent changes the answer, and the runner script never hides a locked question as other answers change. Only the respondent's own change is refused: a locked answer the author's logic already took away before the write (a rule on the date or time, say) is left alone, and when a page's time is up an invalid answer saved in time is kept as saved rather than dropped if dropping it would hide a locked answer (nothing posted is read then, so refusing would stop the respondent for good). A locked answer the author's logic hides keeps counting, since the respondent already saw it graded: the engine scores it with its points and adds its points to the maximum, so the percent stays consistent. `SurveyScoringEngine.CountedQuestions` lists the questions that count (the questions on the respondent's path that logic did not hide, then the locked ones that were hidden or skipped), and the review page shows the same questions. The grading page lists the questions with a maximum (`SurveyAnswer.MaxPoints`), which the engine sets for a locked hidden answer too, and the AI explanation of a quiz result reads each question's points from it. An answer that was not checked and is hidden later is dropped or left out, as before.

## Time limits

`SurveyDefinition.Timing`: `TimeLimitMinutes`, `GraceSeconds` (default 5, kept between 0 and 300 by `SurveyAvailability.GraceSeconds` whatever a recipe or an import stores) and `AutoSubmitAtDeadline`. The deadline is set when the response starts (plus any accommodation's extra minutes, capped at the close time the respondent is held to: the accommodation's, else the survey's, or the link's when that comes first). The close gets the same grace as the deadline, so a last page posted at a deadline cut to the close is still taken. A page may also have its own time limit, counted from the respondent's first visit to the page; a page split into several screens by **Questions per page** gets its limit once, not once per screen. The server enforces both; the runner shows a timer.

A response whose deadline and grace passed is submitted with what was saved in time, by the respondent's next request or by the deadline task. Either way the completion counts toward the survey's per-person limits: the response keeps the key its start was counted under until it completes. The completion rules decide as for a submit; a submission they refuse ends the response **Abandoned** ([Submitting for the respondent](./collecting.md#submitting-for-the-respondent)).

## Grading

`ISurveyGradingService`:

| Method | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `GradeAsync` | Scores questions by hand with comments and overall feedback |
| `RecordScoringHelpAsync` | Marks that a grader used AI help on a question |
| `RegradeAsync` | Scores a response again with its key (its version's, or the one **Regrade versions** last used); grader scores are kept |
| `RegradeVersionsAsync` | Scores responses of older versions with another version's key, only for questions whose type did not change; a question whose key names choices or rows its version lacks keeps its scores and is listed in `SurveyRegradeResult.SkippedQuestions` |
| `ChangeScoreAsync` | Sets the total score; a reason is required |

Every change adds an entry to the response's score history (who, when, from, to, why) and raises `GradedAsync` or `ScoreChangedAsync`.

**Regrade versions** records the key it used on each response (`SurveyResponse.ScoredWithVersionId`; null when that is the response's own version). Every later scoring of the response scores with that key too (`SurveyScoringKeys.ScoringDefinitionAsync`): grading an attempt that still waits for a grader, **Score again**, and finishing a reopened response. So grading a pending essay after a key fix keeps the fixed scores of the other questions, and they stay automatic. The Grade page shows that key's grader guidance. To go back to a version's own key, run **Regrade versions** with that version as the key. A question that kept its own key in the regrade (its key named choices or rows its version lacks) is scored with its own key later too.

Points a grader posts that equal a question's automatic score stay automatic (no `ManualScore`), so a later **Score again** after a key fix still moves them. This is by design: a grader can't pin a score equal to the automatic one, and nothing turns a grader's score back into an automatic one. To fix a total after the fact, use **Change score**, which needs a reason.

Changing a score that is given (**Change score**, **Score again**, saving grades of a graded response, points that differ from a question's automatic or earlier manual score on a response still waiting for a grader, and **Regrade versions**) also asks every `ISurveyScoreChangeCheck` (registered with `services.AddSurveyScoreChangeCheck<T>()`), on top of being allowed to grade the survey; all must allow it (`SurveyScoreChangeChecks.GradingChangesGivenScores`). So a grader who is refused gives points only to the questions that need grading (an answer with no score yet; a score posted unchanged is fine): the other questions' points boxes are read-only for them, and a post that changes one is refused. A grader who is refused sees a graded response without the save button and without the change score card. Learning Assessments adds one that requires **Override grades** on the surveys course items own. The **Regrade Survey Response** workflow task (`RegradeSurveyResponseTask`) runs no score change check: whoever may edit workflows configures it, so give that right only to people who may change grades.

The grading queue (`admin/surveys/grading`) lists responses waiting for a grader in the surveys the user may grade: the global permission, `ISurveyGradingScope` implementations (Learning adds course staff) and `ISurveyAuthorizationService`.

Nobody grades their own work (`SurveyOwnWork`): the queue leaves out the grader's own responses, and the Grade page, its posts (grades, change score, score again), **Regrade versions** (refused when the grader has a response in a chosen version), AI scoring help, and void and delete on the responses pages all refuse a response whose `UserId` is the current user. Void and Delete show only where the user may use them: the Details page and each row of the list ask the same rule as the posts (not their own response, and every score change check when the void or delete changes a given grade, `SurveyScoreChangeChecks.RemovingChangesGivenScore`, which leaves test responses out). The bulk action skips the rows it may not change, counts only the rows it changed, and says how many it left.

Anonymous surveys follow the small-group rule of [Results and reports](./results-and-reports.md#anonymous-surveys) in grading too: the queue leaves out an anonymous survey until its completed responses reach the minimum group size, the Grade page and AI scoring help refuse its responses until then, rows show the response code (never the site-wide number) and the day only, and a queue that lists anonymous responses is in random-id order. The "needs grading" notification is not sent on anonymous surveys, since its time would give away when the person handed in.

Nothing is added to the queue by hand: a completed response appears when it has questions a person must score, such as essays or uploaded files, and leaves once it is graded. Each response waits in one queue only. A module that grades its own responses on its own page registers its context kind with `AddSurveyContextKindGradingQueue(kind)`, and the Surveys queue then leaves those responses out. Learning does this for enrollments, so course quiz attempts wait under **Learning** → **Grading** with coursework. A single survey's queue (`admin/surveys/grading?surveyId=...`) still lists all of its responses.

## Accommodations

An accommodation (`SurveyOverride`) gives a user or a user audience, optionally in one context (such as an enrollment), extra minutes, extra attempts, or another opening and closing time, with a required reason. `ISurveyOverrideService.FindEffectiveAsync` combines the ones that apply into the most generous. They are managed at `admin/surveys/{id}/overrides`. A response keeps what the accommodation granted at its start (`SurveyResponse.Accommodation`: the closing date and extra attempts; the extra minutes are in its deadline) until it ends. The close and the completion look the respondent's accommodation up again when they know the user, so one granted, cut down or revoked while the response is in progress applies at once: with a known user, finding none means none, and the response closes at the survey's date. The kept one is used only when no user is known (the deadline task or upkeep on an anonymous response, which keeps no user), or when an anonymous response keeps only the kind of an identifying context (`SurveyAnonymity.HiddenContextId`), so an accommodation granted for that context can't be found again. So on an anonymous survey, an accommodation revoked or cut down while a response is in progress still applies to the deadline task and to the close upkeep, which use the kept closing date and extra attempts, while the respondent's own requests (a page post, an autosave, a submit) look it up again when they know the user. A finished response keeps no accommodation: the completion clears it, because on an anonymous survey it could single the person out. So when such an anonymous response with a hidden context is reopened (changes after finishing allowed), it gets only an accommodation the lookup finds without the context, such as one granted for the whole survey; extra attempts or a later close granted for the context no longer apply to it, and its finish is held to the survey's own limits and close.
